# Quincy Jones — *Walking in Space* (1969)

## Overview

***Walking in Space*** is a 1969 studio album by the legendary American jazz musician, composer, and producer **Quincy Jones**. Recorded on two sessions over June 18–19, 1969, and released on **A&M/CTI Records**, it stands as one of Jones's most celebrated solo recordings.

The album blends **jazz** and **lounge music**, running approximately **35 minutes** in length. It was produced by Creed Taylor and Quincy Jones, with photography by Pete Turner.

---

## The Story & Context

### A Year of Creative Output (1969)

1969 was an extraordinarily productive year for Quincy Jones. Alongside *Walking in Space*, he worked on soundtracks for:

- ***The Lost Man*** (1969)
- ***Bob & Carol & Ted & Alice*** (1969)

*Walking in Space* represented his desire to create a definitive, cohesive jazz album — one that showcased the very best musicians of the era in a relaxed, sophisticated setting.

### The *Hair* Connection

Two tracks on the album derive from the groundbreaking 1967 rock musical ***Hair***:

- **"Dead End"** — an arrangement of Galt MacDermot's composition
- **"Walking in Space"** (the title track) — also by MacDermot, Rado, and Ragni

The title track features a **vocal solo by Valerie Simpson**, who had just joined the group The Supremes' successor acts. Jones loved the song and brought her in to add a soulful, contemporary vocal layer to what was originally a rock/jazz piece.

### Conceptual Vision

Jones envisioned *Walking in Space* as a "space album" — not literally about outer space, but about **walking through musical space**. The title track's extended improvisation (12+ minutes) reflects this concept: musicians "walking" through harmonic and melodic territory together.

## Recording Details

- **Label:** A&M/CTI Records
- **Producers:** Creed Taylor and Quincy Jones

- **Total Length:** ~35:06

The recording sessions were relatively short, suggesting that Jones had carefully planned the arrangements and knew exactly what he wanted from each musician. The tight timeframe indicates a confident, well-rehearsed approach.

## Reception & Critical Acclaim

***Walking in Space*** received **extraordinary critical praise**:

- **AllMusic** — ★★★★★ (5/5 stars)
- **DownBeat Magazine** (March 1970) — ★★★★½ (4.5/5 stars)

The album is remembered as a landmark of late-1960s jazz, showcasing the transition from hard bop to more fusion-influenced, electric instrumentation while maintaining sophisticated arrangements.

---

## Legacy & Significance

### Why It Matters

1. **Jazz Meets Electric:** The inclusion of electric guitar (Eric Gale), electric piano (Bob James), and bass guitar alongside acoustic instruments reflects the era's musical evolution.

2. **All-Star Collaborations:** Jones brought together musicians who were at or near their peaks, creating a unique sonic document.

3. **The "Space" Concept:** The album's title and concept — exploring vast harmonic and improvisational territories — anticipated later fusion and jazz-rock movements.

4. **Cultural Moment:** Recorded in 1969, the album captures a pivotal moment in music when boundaries between jazz, rock, soul, and pop were dissolving.

5. **Jones's Artistic Identity:** While Jones was better known as a producer (for Michael Jackson, Earth Wind & Fire, etc.), *Walking in Space* established his credentials as a brilliant bandleader and composer.

### Notable Track: "Walking in Space"

The title track is the album's centerpiece — a **12-minute improvisational journey** featuring:
- Extended solos from Freddie Hubbard (trumpet), J.J. Johnson (trombone), and Hubert Laws (flute)
- Valerie Simpson's soulful vocal performance on top of the jazz instrumentation
- A seamless blend of rock-derived melody with sophisticated jazz harmony

This track exemplifies the album's ambition: to create something that felt both contemporary (rock influence) and timeless (jazz tradition).

## Discographic Context

*Walking in Space* appears in Quincy Jones's discography between two other 1969 releases:
- ***The Lost Man*** (1969) — film soundtrack
- ***Bob & Carol & Ted & Alice*** (1969) — film soundtrack

It also features prominently in **Freddie Hubbard's** discography under "With Quincy Jones."

## Fun Facts

- The album is **not to be confused** with the term "extra-vehicular activity" (EVA) — though the space theme is clearly intentional.
- **Toots Thielemans**, the Belgian guitarist and harmonica player, was one of the most versatile musicians in jazz history. His presence on this album adds a unique European flavor.
- **Ray Brown** (bass) was married to Ella Fitzgerald for 50 years and was considered the greatest bassist in jazz history.
- **Freddie Hubbard** was at the peak of his powers in 1969, known for his powerful, emotional trumpet playing.

---

## Summary

***Walking in Space*** is Quincy Jones's crowning achievement as a solo artist — an album that brought together the finest musicians of the era to explore the boundaries between jazz, rock, and soul. Its critical acclaim (5-star AllMusic rating, 4.5-star DownBeat review) endures, and it remains a essential listen for anyone interested in late-1960s jazz and Quincy Jones's artistry beyond his legendary production work.

The album captures a perfect moment in music history — when innovation and tradition coexisted, when electric instruments met acoustic mastery, and when Quincy Jones proved he was not just a producer, but a visionary bandleader and composer in his own right.
